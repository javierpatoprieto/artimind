Two brands from different worlds, one visual language. This is a concept campaign — no client, no brief — produced to answer a single question: can AI hold a campaign together across five frames and a film, the way a real shoot does?

## The system

Everything started with one image: a martini glass resting on the strings of a red tennis racket. That collision — a fragile object where it shouldn't be — set the rules for the rest.

- **Palette.** Crocodile green, cream, lacquer red, silver. Nothing else enters the frame.
- **Light.** Low hard sun, long shadows. Not a single frame with soft light.
- **Era.** 1972, shot on film, Ektachrome grain.
- **Test.** If a frame works in black and white, it isn't finished.

## The sequence

Hero still, muse, world, detail, closing frame. The series moves from object to person to place and back out to the empty court at sunset — the campaign's own aftermath. The last frame carries no product at all, which is exactly why it works.

## The film

A 15-second spot closes the campaign. Every cut is motivated by a sound: a cocktail pick against crystal, a distant ball, one upright bass note. When the sound tells you where to cut, the edit feels inevitable — even though no camera ever existed.

## The workflow

Stills art directed and finished in the Magnific suite, motion in Seedance. The tools render frames; the decisions — palette, hour of day, period, what gets rejected — are the work. Consistency is not a bonus in a campaign. It is the requirement.
